**UGC NET December 2026 registration is open till 28 October 2026 (11:50 PM).** The exam is on **14 to 18 and 21 December 2026** in 87 subjects, as a computer-based test. If you have a Master's degree with 55% (50% for reserved categories), or you're in the final year of one, you can apply.

## What UGC NET qualifies you for

Your result puts you in one of three categories, based on your marks:

| Category | JRF (fellowship) | Assistant Professor | PhD admission |
|---|---|---|---|
| Category 1: JRF + Assistant Professor | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Category 2: Assistant Professor + PhD | No | Yes | Yes |
| Category 3: PhD admission only | No | No | Yes |

JRF is the one most people want, because it comes with a monthly fellowship for PhD research. It's also the hardest, and it's the only one with an age limit.

## Eligibility

### Educational qualification

- **Master's degree** (or equivalent) in the subject, from a UGC-recognised university, with at least **55% marks**. For OBC-NCL, SC, ST, PwD/PwBD and third gender candidates, it's **50%**. No rounding off: 54.9% is not 55%.
- **Final-year PG students** and those waiting for results can apply. You'll be admitted provisionally, and you must pass your Master's with the required marks within **2 years** of the NET result (within 1 year if you qualify only for PhD admission). Otherwise you're disqualified.
- **4-year bachelor's degree** holders need **75%** (5% relaxation for reserved categories). You can apply for JRF and PhD admission, but **not** for Assistant Professor. You can choose any subject you want to do a PhD in, whatever your degree was in.
- PhD holders whose Master's was completed by 19 September 1991 get a 5% relaxation (55% to 50%).

Write the exam in your PG subject. If your subject isn't in NTA's list of 87, pick the closest related one.

### Age limit

| Purpose | Age limit |
|---|---|
| JRF | **Not more than 30 years** as on 1 December 2026 |
| Assistant Professor | No upper age limit |
| PhD admission | No upper age limit |

JRF age relaxation (total capped at 5 years):

- **Up to 5 years:** OBC-NCL (central list), SC, ST, PwD/PwBD, third gender and **women**
- **Up to 5 years:** research experience in your PG subject (not done as part of a degree), with a certificate
- **3 years:** LLM degree holders
- **Up to 5 years:** service in the armed forces

So a woman of any category can try for JRF up to 35. If you're over the limit, you can still write NET for Assistant Professor or PhD.

## Exam pattern and marking

One 3-hour sitting with **no break** between the two papers:

| Paper | What it tests | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Teaching and research aptitude: reasoning, comprehension, general awareness | 50 | 100 |
| Paper 2 | Your subject | 100 | 200 |
| **Total** | | **150** | **300** |

- Each question carries **2 marks**.
- **No negative marking.** Answer every question.
- Papers are **bilingual, in English and Hindi** (language papers are in that language). Still choose your medium carefully on the form.
- Syllabus for every subject is on the UGC site ([ugcnetonline.in](https://www.ugcnetonline.in/syllabus-new.php)). NTA doesn't send it.

### How the cut-off works

NTA doesn't publish a fixed pass mark. Only the **top 6%** of candidates who write both papers (on the basis of a Master's degree) are declared eligible for Assistant Professor, and those slots are split by category and subject.

To even be considered, you need **at least 40% aggregate** (General and EWS) or **35%** (all reserved categories) across both papers. That's 120 or 105 out of 300. Think of it as the floor, not the target. The real cut-off is usually much higher.

## How to apply for UGC NET December 2026

1. Go to [ugcnet.nta.nic.in](https://ugcnet.nta.nic.in) and click **"UGC-NET Dec 2026 Application form"**.
2. **Register** with your own (or a parent's) mobile number and email. You can also sign in through Meri Pehchan.
3. Use **Aadhaar verification** if you can. NTA says it makes things easier at the exam centre and later when your JRF is processed.
4. Fill in your details, PG marks, subject, medium (English or Hindi) and 4 exam cities.
5. Upload your photo and signature as per the size rules in the bulletin.
6. Pay the fee and **download the confirmation page**.

Submit **only one** application. NTA's bulletin is at [ugcnet.nta.nic.in/information-bulletin](https://ugcnet.nta.nic.in/information-bulletin/). Read the photo and signature rules there before you start, since that's where most forms go wrong.

Helpline: 011-40759000 / 011-69227700, ugcnet@nta.ac.in.

## How to use the 2 months before the exam

You have about 8 weeks from today. Here's a plan that works for most people:

- **Weeks 1 to 3: Paper 1.** It's 100 marks and the same for every subject. Teaching aptitude, research aptitude, comprehension, communication, reasoning, data interpretation, ICT, people and environment, and higher education system. It's the easiest place to gain marks.
- **Weeks 2 to 6: Paper 2,** unit by unit from the UGC syllabus. Your PG notes cover most of it, but NET also asks about thinkers, books and dates, so make short lists as you go.
- **Weeks 6 to 8: previous papers.** NTA's earlier question papers are the best guide to the level. Do them timed, 3 hours, no break, because that's how the real exam runs.

Since there's no negative marking, never leave a question blank. If you're stuck, eliminate what you can and pick one.

## Should you apply?

Yes, if you have a PG degree (or will finish one soon) and see yourself in teaching or research. ₹1,150 is the most you'll pay, and even Category 3 (PhD admission only) opens doors at many universities.

Be realistic about JRF, though. Only a small part of the qualified candidates get it, so plan for more than one attempt if JRF is your goal.
