Tesla has finally shown the new Roadster, 9 years after it was first teased. It comes in 3 versions, and the top one, called **Rocket**, uses air thrusters to lift off the ground for short flights. Tesla claims **0-60 mph in under 0.59 seconds** and **more than 300 miles (480 km) of range**.

The Tesla Roadster 2026 reveal happened on the night of 15 October in Texas, which was around **6:25 am on 16 October in India**. There's no final price yet, production is planned for 2028, and there's **no India launch announced**.

## What Tesla actually showed on stage

This is the part that got everyone talking. During the demo, as described by Tesla and reporters at the event:

- The car drove up a curved ramp until it was **upside down**, then flew off, flipped and landed on the road.
- It **hovered over a row of Tesla vehicles** (Cybercab, Cybertruck, Semi, Model Y and Model 3).
- It did a 0-60 mph run in **0.81 seconds**, the time shown in Tesla's own 0-60 clip.

After the lift-off, Musk said, "Flying cars are real." He also joked about trying Evel Knievel's famous Snake River Canyon jump.

How does it stick upside down? Tesla says a **fan and ground-sealing system** sucks air from under the car. That creates so much downforce that the car can hold onto a ceiling-like surface, even when it's not moving. Tesla says the thrusters produce 4 to 5 times the force the tyres can.

## The 3 versions, explained simply

**Performance.** The "normal" Roadster, if you can call it that. Two seats, active wings, the suction fan system and a Track Mode that lowers the car. Tesla calls it road-ready. 0-60 mph in under 1.6 seconds.

**Thrust.** Adds cold-gas thrusters at the back. These push the car forward much harder than tyres alone. 0-60 in under 0.9 seconds. Tesla calls it race-ready, with a limited release.

**Rocket.** Single seat, with thrusters all around the body for lift and turning in the air. This is the "flying" one. 0-60 in under 0.59 seconds. It is **invite only, not available in all regions and not approved for public road use**.

The thrusters run on compressed air stored in carbon-fibre tanks. No rocket fuel, no fire. Tesla uses magnesium, titanium and carbon fibre to keep weight down, and the car has a steer-by-wire yoke instead of a round steering wheel.

## Why this matters (and why some people aren't impressed)

Honestly, the Roadster won't change Tesla's business. It's a very expensive, low-volume car. Most of Tesla's money still comes from the Model 3 and Model Y, and its big bets are robotaxis and the Optimus robot.

What the Roadster does is show off engineering. The suction fans and thrusters are real tech, and the demo clips are going viral for a reason.

But here's the sceptic's view, and it's fair:

- Tesla first promised this car in **2017**, with 0-60 in 1.9 seconds and 620 miles of range. Buyers paid reservations then too. It's now 2026 and production is still 2 years away.
- The range is now "over 300 miles", about half of what was promised in 2017.
- All speed and range numbers are **Tesla's own claims**. No independent test yet.
- Musk said the car on stage "will be the actual car that's on the roads", but Tesla's own page says the Rocket is not road-approved.

So: amazing demo, but wait for real deliveries before believing every number.

## What it means for you in India

**Can you buy it here?** Not now. Tesla sells the Model Y in India, but it has not listed the Roadster on its India site or given a rupee price.

**Even if it comes,** an imported car like this would attract heavy import duty in India. Just the US reservation for the Performance version (US$50,000) is more than the full price of most cars sold in India. This is a car to watch on YouTube, not to plan for.

**For engineering students,** this is a good case study. Downforce from ground-effect fans, cold-gas thrusters, steer-by-wire and lightweight materials all come straight from motorsport and aerospace. If you're in mechanical, automobile or aerospace engineering, watch the full event once and note how they explain the tech.

**For people who follow Tesla stock,** reports say the shares rose in early US trading after the reveal. Tesla's next big date is its Q3 results, reported to be on 21 October.
