**The APPSC Group 2 notification 2026 is out, with 720 posts.** It is Notification No. 37/2026, dated 10 November. You can apply online on psc.ap.gov.in from **10 November to 11 December 2026** (11:59 pm). The screening test is fixed for **21 February 2027**.

Any graduate can apply for most posts. The single biggest chunk is **203 Deputy Tahsildar posts**, the post most Group 2 aspirants in Andhra Pradesh want.

## Zones and local reservation

Post codes 01 to 07 (all Executive posts) are **zonal posts**. Post code 12 is a district post. For these, 95% of seats go to local candidates of that zone or district, under the Presidential Order 2025.

Post codes 08 to 11 and 13 to 30 are **state cadre**, so local reservation doesn't apply there. Candidates from other states can apply, but they get no local or caste reservation and must pay the full fee.

## APPSC Group 2 eligibility

**Qualification (as on 10 November 2026):**

- **Most posts (codes 01 to 10, 14 to 30):** a bachelor's degree from a UGC-recognised university. For Sub-Registrar, a law degree gets preference.
- **Senior Accountant (11, 12) and Junior Accountant (13):** a degree (Commerce, Economics or Maths preferred, but any degree is accepted) **plus** one SBTET AP certificate course (Office Automation, PC Maintenance or Web Designing). A BCA, B.Sc/B.Com/B.A (Computers), or B.Tech/B.E in CSE or IT also counts instead of the certificate.

**Age:** 18 to 44 years as on **1 July 2026** for most posts. The exceptions are in the table above (Excise SI 18–30, Sub-Registrar 20–44, Municipal Commissioner 21–44).

**Age relaxation:**

| Category | Relaxation |
|---|---|
| SC, ST, BC, EWS | 5 years |
| SC/ST carried-forward (limited) vacancies | 10 years |
| Persons with Benchmark Disabilities | 10 years |
| Ex-servicemen, NCC instructors | Service period + 3 years |
| Regular AP state govt employees | Length of service, up to 5 years |

**Physical standards (Excise SI only):** men need 165 cm height and an 81 cm chest with 5 cm expansion. Women need 152.5 cm height and at least 45.5 kg weight. Both need 6/6 distant vision in each eye. Flat foot, knock knees and colour blindness disqualify you.

## Selection process and exam pattern

There are three steps:

1. **Screening test** (offline, OMR), on 21 February 2027
2. **Main exam** (computer based, objective), date to be announced
3. **Computer Proficiency Test (CPT)**, only qualifying

Candidates for the Main exam are picked at **50 times the number of vacancies**, so about 36,000 people move on from screening. Final selection is on Main exam marks only. Screening marks are not added.

| Stage | Paper | Questions | Time | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Screening | General Studies and Mental Ability | 150 | 150 min | 150 |
| Main | Paper I: Social History of AP + Indian Constitution | 150 | 150 min | 150 |
| Main | Paper II: Indian and AP Economy + Science and Technology | 150 | 150 min | 150 |
| CPT | Office automation (practical) | — | 60 min | 100 (qualifying) |

- **Negative marking:** 1/3 of a mark is cut for every wrong answer, in both screening and Main.
- **Minimum marks in Main:** 40% for OC and EWS, 35% for BC, 30% for SC, ST and PwBD.
- **CPT pass marks:** 40 (OC), 35 (BC), 30 (SC, ST, PwBD) out of 100.

The screening paper has five 30-mark sections: Indian History, Geography, Indian Society, Current Affairs and Mental Ability.

## How to prepare in 100 days

You have roughly 14 weeks until 21 February. Here's how I would use them.

- **Weeks 1–6: the two big static sections.** Indian History and Geography (India and AP) make up 60 of the 150 marks. Use AP state textbooks (Class 6–10 Social) first, then one standard book.
- **Weeks 7–10: Indian Society and Mental Ability.** Society is mostly about welfare schemes, constitutional safeguards and social issues, so it overlaps with current affairs. Practise reasoning every day. It's the easiest section to score full marks in.
- **Weeks 11–14: current affairs and mocks.** Cover national, international and AP news from about March 2026. Take at least 8 full OMR mock tests with the 1/3 negative marking.

Don't start Main exam (Paper I and II) preparation seriously before screening, but keep reading AP economy and the Socio-Economic Survey alongside. It helps in both.

## How to apply for APPSC Group 2 2026

1. Go to **psc.ap.gov.in**.
2. If you're new, complete **One Time Profile Registration (OTPR)**. Your user ID is sent to your mobile and email. If you already have an OTPR ID (from Group 1, ASO or EO), update it first, because your fee and age relaxation are filled in from it.
3. Log in with your OTPR ID and open Notification 37/2026.
4. Pick your posts and give **three exam-centre preferences** for both screening and Main.
5. Pay the fee and submit. Download the filled form PDF.

After 11 December, a **7-day correction window** opens at ₹100 per change. Your name, fee category and age relaxation can't be changed at all, so check your OTPR before you pay.

The full notification PDF is on [psc.ap.gov.in](https://psc.ap.gov.in/Documents/NotificationDocuments/Group_II_Detailed_Notification_37_2026.pdf).

## Should you apply?

If you're an AP graduate under 44 and preparing for any state exam, yes. 720 posts is a big batch, and 203 Deputy Tahsildar seats is more than most aspirants will see again for a while.

The screening syllabus overlaps heavily with [APPSC Group 1](/appsc-group-1-recruitment-2026) and the [APPSC ASO exam](/appsc-aso-notification-2026), so preparation for one helps the other. Graduates with a computer degree have an extra edge for the 138 accountant posts, which ask for that qualification.

Skip the Excise SI preference if you're over 30 or don't meet the height standard. You would only waste a preference.

Your next step: open psc.ap.gov.in today and check that your OTPR details (caste, local status, white card) are correct, then apply well before 11 December. The site usually slows down in the last 3 days.
