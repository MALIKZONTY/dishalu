**APPSC ASO notification 2026 is out for 196 Assistant Statistical Officer posts.** Online applications open on **13 October** and close on **2 November 2026** (11:59 pm). You need a degree with Statistics in it, either as a main subject or as a paper inside a Maths, Economics, Commerce or Computer Science degree.

One thing to know before you read on. APPSC has released only the brief notification so far (No. 20/2026, dated 15 September). The detailed one, with age limit, fee and zone-wise vacancies, is due **on or before 13 October**. I've marked clearly below what is confirmed and what is from the last cycle.

This is the *Statistical* Officer post. It's not the Assistant Section Officer post that comes under Group 2.

## Why 196 posts is a big deal

The last time APPSC advertised this post, in March 2024 (Notification No. 12/2024), there were **5** carried-forward vacancies. This time there are 196.

Posts for statistics graduates don't come often in AP, and almost never in this number. If you qualify, this is the one to take seriously this year.

## APPSC ASO notification 2026: eligibility

You must hold one of these from a recognised university:

- Bachelor's degree with **Statistics** as one of the main subjects, or
- Bachelor's degree with **Mathematics** as a main subject, with Statistics as a paper in one, two or all three years, or
- Bachelor's degree with **Economics** as a main subject, with Statistics as a paper in at least one year, or
- Bachelor's degree with **Commerce** as a main subject, with Statistics as a paper in at least one year, or
- Bachelor's degree with **Computer Science** as a main subject, with Statistics as a paper in at least one year.

So a BCom or BSc Computers graduate can apply, but only if Statistics shows up as a paper on the marks memo. Check your memos before you pay the fee. A BTech, or a BA with no Statistics paper, doesn't fit the wording above.

**Important:** you must have the qualification as on the date of the detailed notification. If your final-year result isn't out by then, you aren't eligible this time.

### Age limit

Not announced for 2026 yet. In the 2024 notification it was **18 to 42 years** as on 1 July of that year, with 5 years extra for SC, ST, BC and EWS candidates and 10 years for persons with benchmark disabilities.

This year's [APPSC Group 1 notification](/appsc-group-1-recruitment-2026) uses 18 to 44 years, so the upper limit here may also be higher. That's my guess, not an official figure. I'll update this page when the detailed PDF is out.

## Selection process and exam pattern

There are two steps, and APPSC has confirmed there's **no screening test**, whatever the number of applicants.

### Step 1: Written exam (computer-based, degree standard)

| Paper | Subject | Questions | Time | Marks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper I | General Studies & Mental Ability | 150 | 150 minutes | 150 |
| Paper II | Subject | 150 | 150 minutes | 150 |
| | **Total** | | | **300** |

- Objective type, taken on a computer at an exam centre.
- **Negative marking:** one-third of the question's marks is cut for each wrong answer.
- If the exam runs in more than one session, APPSC will normalise the marks.

### Step 2: Computer Proficiency Test (qualifying)

Candidates shortlisted on written-exam merit take a practical test in office software. It's 60 minutes for 100 marks. You only need to cross the qualifying mark: **40 for OC, 35 for BC, 30 for SC, ST and PBD**.

The test has a short multiple-choice part (20% of marks). The rest is practical work: a formatted Word document with tables and mail merge, typing speed, an Excel sheet with formulas and charts, and a PowerPoint presentation.

## Syllabus in short

**Paper I, General Studies & Mental Ability:** current events (international, national and Andhra Pradesh), general science and technology, Indian history with focus on AP and the national movement, geography of India and AP, polity and governance, Indian economy and planning, sustainable development, disaster management, logical reasoning, and basic data analysis (tables, charts, mean, median, mode, variance).

**Paper II, Subject.** This is the surprise for many people. It isn't only statistics. The 10 units are:

1. Economic concepts (demand, elasticity, markets, national income, money, banking, inflation, public finance)
2. International economics (trade, balance of payments, exchange rates, IMF and World Bank)
3. Indian economics (population, poverty, unemployment, planning, NITI Aayog, fiscal and monetary policy)
4. Financial accounting (journal, ledger, cash book, trial balance, final accounts, depreciation)
5. Basics of computers (binary, octal and hexadecimal systems, flow charts, spreadsheets)
6. Introduction to statistics (data collection, sampling, probability, frequency distribution, diagrams)
7. Measures of central tendency
8. Measures of dispersion and skewness
9. Correlation
10. Time series and index numbers

Half the paper is economics, accounting and computers. A Statistics graduate has to pick up accounting. A Commerce graduate has to revise statistics. Nobody walks in fully ready, which evens things out.

## Pay scale

The notified scale is **₹37,640 to ₹1,15,500**. Your basic pay starts at ₹37,640, and DA, HRA and other allowances are added as per AP government rules. I'm not quoting an in-hand figure because it changes with your posting place and the DA rate at the time.

## Application fee

Not announced for 2026 yet. In 2024 it was ₹250 processing fee plus ₹80 exam fee, and SC, ST, BC, PBD, ex-servicemen and AP white ration card families paid only the ₹250. This year's Group 1 notification charges ₹250 plus ₹120, so expect something close to that.

## How to apply

1. Go to [psc.ap.gov.in](https://psc.ap.gov.in). If you've never applied to APPSC, do the **One Time Profile Registration (OTPR)** first. It gives you an OTPR ID. Registering alone isn't an application.
2. From 13 October, log in with your OTPR ID and open the online application for Notification No. 20/2026.
3. Check the details pulled in from your OTPR profile. Name, community and local status decide your fee and age relaxation, so fix mistakes in the OTPR profile before you submit.
4. Fill in your qualification and choose exam centres.
5. Pay the fee online and download the submitted application.

Do the OTPR today if you don't have one. It's the step that goes wrong most often, and you don't need to wait for the 13th.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Applying without Statistics on your memo.** Your application can be rejected at certificate verification, even after you clear the exam.
- **Reading only the brief notification.** The zone-wise vacancies and category break-up come in the detailed one. In 2024 this was a zonal post, so your local zone mattered.
- **Guessing in the exam.** With one-third negative marking, four wrong answers wipe out more than one right one.
- **Ignoring the computer test.** It's only qualifying, but if you fail it you aren't appointed.

## Should you apply?

Yes, if your degree fits. The eligibility rule keeps out most graduates, so the crowd is far smaller than for Group 2 or Group 4. And 196 posts against 5 last time is a gap you may not see again for years.

Think twice if you're banking on Statistics alone. Paper II needs economics and accounting too, and Paper I is a full general studies paper. Start with the units you've never studied.
