## Posts and salary

SSC CHSL fills three types of posts in central government ministries and offices.

![North Block in New Delhi, part of the Central Secretariat](/images/posts/ssc-chsl-2026-last-date-2.webp)
*North Block, New Delhi, home to several central ministries. Photo: Mukulfaiz / Wikimedia, [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?curid=18505723)*

| Post | Pay Level | Pay scale |
|---|---|---|
| Lower Division Clerk (LDC) / Junior Secretariat Assistant (JSA) | Level 2 | ₹19,900 – ₹63,200 |
| Data Entry Operator (DEO) | Level 4 or 5 | ₹25,500 – ₹81,100 / ₹29,200 – ₹92,300 |
| Data Entry Operator Grade 'A' | Level 4 | ₹25,500 – ₹81,100 |

On top of basic pay you get DA, HRA (10% to 30% depending on the city) and transport allowance. So the in-hand salary is clearly higher than the basic pay, and it's higher in big cities than in small towns.

## SSC CHSL 2026 eligibility

### Age limit

**18 to 27 years as on 1 August 2026.** That means you should be born **not before 2 August 1999** and **not after 1 August 2008**.

Age relaxation:

- SC/ST: 5 years
- OBC: 3 years
- PwBD and ex-servicemen: extra relaxation as per the notification

### Educational qualification

You must have **passed 12th (or equivalent) on or before 7 October 2026**.

For some **DEO posts in specific departments**, you need **12th with Science and Maths**. If you're from Arts or Commerce, you can still apply for LDC/JSA and the other DEO posts. Check the department list in the notification before choosing your post preferences.

## Application fee

| Category | Fee |
|---|---|
| General / OBC / EWS (men) | ₹100 |
| Women (all categories), SC, ST, PwBD, ex-servicemen | No fee |

Fee payment closed on 8 October, 11 PM. An application without the fee payment doesn't count, so check your status in your SSC login if you paid near the deadline.

## How to apply for SSC CHSL 2026

The whole thing takes 20 to 30 minutes if you keep your documents ready.

1. Go to [ssc.gov.in](https://ssc.gov.in/).
2. **Register first (One-Time Registration).** If you already registered for any SSC exam on the new website, just log in.
3. Log in and open the **CHSL 2026** application under the "Apply" section.
4. Fill in your personal details, qualification and category exactly as on your certificates.
5. Choose your **exam centre preferences** and **post preferences**.
6. **Capture your live photograph** using your webcam or phone camera when the form asks for it. Use good light and a plain background.
7. **Upload your signature** in the required size (JPEG, around 10 to 20 KB).
8. Preview everything carefully, then submit.
9. **Pay the fee** if it applies to you, and download or print the final application.

### Keep these ready before you start

- Aadhaar or another photo ID
- 10th certificate (for name and date of birth)
- 12th marksheet
- Category / PwBD / EWS certificate, if you're claiming it
- A scanned signature
- A working email and phone number for OTPs

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Name or date of birth not matching your 10th certificate.** This causes trouble at document verification later.
- **A dark or blurred live photo.** The system may reject it, or it can cause issues at the exam centre.
- **Leaving the fee unpaid.** Many people submit the form and forget to pay before the fee deadline.
- **Picking exam centres randomly.** Choose centres you can actually reach on the exam day.
If you did make a mistake, the **correction window is 14 to 16 October 2026 (till 11 PM)**. SSC charges **₹200 for the first correction** and **₹500 for the second**, whatever your category. After 16 October, no changes are allowed at all.

## Selection process and exam pattern

SSC CHSL has two stages:

1. **Tier 1:** a computer-based objective exam
2. **Tier 2:** a harder computer-based exam, which also includes a **typing or skill test**

![Hands typing on a white computer keyboard on a dark desk](/images/posts/ssc-chsl-2026-last-date-3.webp)
*Typing speed matters in Tier 2, so start practising early. Photo: Rawpixel, [CC0](https://www.rawpixel.com/image/5916691/image-phone-public-domain-hand)*

### Tier 1 exam pattern

| Section | Questions | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| General Intelligence (Reasoning) | 25 | 50 |
| General Awareness | 25 | 50 |
| Quantitative Aptitude | 25 | 50 |
| English Language | 25 | 50 |
| **Total** | **100** | **200** |

- Time: **60 minutes** (80 minutes for eligible PwBD candidates)
- Each correct answer: **+2**
- Each wrong answer: **−0.50**

Tier 1 is mainly a screening stage. Your final selection depends mostly on your Tier 2 performance and the skill test.

## Should you apply?

Honestly, if you're eligible, **yes, apply**. The fee is ₹100 or nothing, and it's a permanent central government job with regular promotions.

A few things to keep in mind:

- **Competition is high.** Lakhs apply for about 2,500 posts. With Tier 1 from 30 November, start preparing now, not after the admit card comes.
- **Typing matters.** Many people clear Tier 2 written but struggle in the skill test. Practise typing 15 to 20 minutes every day from now.
- **It pairs well with other exams.** The same reasoning, maths and English prep helps for SSC CGL, MTS and bank clerk exams. If you're also writing bank exams, see our [IBPS Clerk admit card 2026 guide](/ibps-clerk-admit-card-2026).
