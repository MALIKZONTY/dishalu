You can raise objections only once, so do your score calculation first and list every doubtful question before you pay.

## A small date confusion to know about

The official press note (Ltr. No. 733/TG-SET/OU/26) says the challenge window closes at **11:00 PM** on 15 October. The notice on the apply portal says **11:50 PM**. Don't test which one the server follows. Treat 11:00 PM as the deadline, and better still, finish by the evening of the 14th, because these portals slow down badly on the last night.

## How to download the TG SET answer key 2026

1. Open [tgset.aptonline.in](https://tgset.aptonline.in/TGSET/HomePage.aspx). You can also reach it from the "Apply Online" link on telanganaset.org.
2. Click **Download of Response Sheet**. Enter your registration number, TG SET hall ticket number and date of birth (dd/mm/yyyy).
3. Save your response sheet. It shows the Question ID of every question you got and the option you marked.
4. Go back and open **Master Question Papers** for your subject, exam day and session. Download the master paper with the preliminary key.
5. Keep both PDFs side by side. You need both to check your answers.

## Why you need the master question paper

This is where most people go wrong. In the CBT, the questions and options were **jumbled for every candidate**. Question 12 on your screen may be question 47 in someone else's paper, and option (B) for you may be option (D) for them.

So don't match by question number. Match by **Question ID**. Find the Question ID in your response sheet, search for the same ID in the master question paper, and compare your marked option with the key there. The official guidelines say objections must be raised using the options as given in the master paper, not as they appeared on your screen.

## How to calculate your TG SET score

| Paper | Questions | Marks each | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper I (teaching and research aptitude, common for all) | 50 | 2 | 100 |
| Paper II (your subject) | 100 | 2 | 200 |
| **Total** | **150** | | **300** |

Count your correct answers in both papers and multiply by 2. The official scheme doesn't mention negative marking, so a wrong answer simply earns zero.

Example: 32 correct in Paper I and 58 correct in Paper II gives you (32 + 58) × 2 = **180 out of 300**, or 60%.

## What score is enough to qualify?

There are two filters, and you must clear both.

**Filter 1: minimum aggregate.** You must have taken both papers and scored at least:

- **40% (120/300)** for General (Unreserved) candidates
- **35% (105/300)** for SC, ST, BC, PwD and transgender candidates

**Filter 2: the 6% rule.** Only **6% of the candidates who appeared in both papers** are declared qualified. These slots are split by subject and then by category as per Telangana's reservation policy (36% unreserved, 10% EWS, 10% ST, BC-A 7%, BC-B 10%, BC-C 1%, BC-D 7%, BC-E 4%, and SC Groups I, II and III at 1%, 9% and 5%). Within each category, 33.3% of slots are for women and 5% are for persons with disability.

So the real cut-off is the score of the last person who fits inside your subject's slot for your category. In crowded subjects like Commerce or English, it usually sits well above the 40% line. Candidates from outside Telangana are treated as Unreserved, whatever their category at home.

## How to raise an objection

1. Read the guidelines on the portal and tick that you've understood them.
2. Open **Objections on Preliminary Key** and log in.
3. Enter the Question ID, the answer you think is correct, and your justification. Attach proof (a textbook page, a standard reference, an official source) as a PDF or JPG.
4. Add **all** your objections in one go. The portal allows objections on any number of questions, but only **once**. You can't come back and add more.
5. Pay ₹200 per question online and download the objection print. You can check the payment under "Key Objections Fee Payment Status".

Objections without proper justification are rejected straight away. If yours is accepted, the ₹200 comes back within 7 working days of the result. Nobody is told individually whether their objection was accepted; you'll only see it in the final key.

## Is it worth challenging?

Only when you're sure and you have proof. ₹200 per question adds up quickly, and "I think it's B" with no reference is rejected.

Think about where you stand too. If you're 2 or 3 questions short of what you guess your subject's cut-off will be, a solid objection could change your result. If you're 30 marks short, don't spend ₹1,000 on objections. Spend that time on the next cycle or on UGC NET.

And remember, if an answer is changed, it's changed for **everyone**, not only for the person who raised it.

## What happens next

1. Objections close on 15 October.
2. A panel of subject experts reviews every challenge and the key is revised where needed.
3. The final key is published (look for "Master Question Papers with Final Key" on the portal).
4. The result is prepared from the final key. No date has been announced.
5. Qualified candidates go through certificate verification, where you show original certificates. TG SET qualification makes you eligible for Assistant Professor and Lecturer posts in Telangana universities and colleges.
