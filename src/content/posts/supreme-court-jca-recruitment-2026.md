**Supreme Court JCA recruitment 2026 is out for 250 Junior Court Assistant posts.** Online applications open on **7 October 2026** and close on **28 October 2026 at 11:55 pm**. Any graduate aged 18 to 30 who can type 35 words per minute in English can apply.

It's a Level 6 central job (basic pay ₹35,400) in the Supreme Court Registry in New Delhi. The first exam is tentatively on **20 November 2026**, so you get about 6 weeks to prepare after you apply.

## Supreme Court JCA 2026 eligibility

### Qualification

You need two things, both as on **1 September 2026**:

- A **bachelor's degree** from a recognised university in India. Any stream works: BA, BCom, BSc, BTech, BBA.
- **Knowledge of computer operation with a typing speed of 35 words per minute** on a computer.

There's no minimum percentage. Final-year students aren't mentioned, and the cut-off date is 1 September 2026, so you should have your degree by then.

### Age limit

You must be **at least 18 and not more than 30 years old on 1 September 2026**.

| Category | Upper age limit |
|---|---|
| General | 30 years |
| OBC (non-creamy layer), SC, ST, PwD, ex-servicemen | Relaxation as per Government of India rules |
| Dependents of freedom fighters | Up to 5 years extra (up to 35) |
| Departmental candidates of the Supreme Court Registry | No upper age limit |
| Employees of other govt departments, PSUs, banks | **No relaxation** |

The advertisement doesn't list the exact relaxation years for each category. Under the usual central rules, OBC gets 3 years, SC/ST 5 years and PwD 10 years, but go by what the application form allows.

## Vacancies and salary

The advertisement gives **250 tentative vacancies**. It doesn't give a category-wise split. Reservation follows Rule 4A of the Supreme Court Officers and Servants Rules, 1961. The Registry can also increase or reduce the number later.

JCA is in **Level 6 of the Pay Matrix**, with an initial basic pay of **₹35,400 a month**. Allowances like DA, HRA and transport allowance are added on top, so the gross salary is clearly higher than the basic. The posting is in the Supreme Court in New Delhi.

## Supreme Court JCA exam pattern 2026

There are 4 stages, and you must cross the minimum marks in each one.

| Stage | What it is | Time | Marks | Minimum to qualify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Objective type test (100 MCQs) | 1.5 hours | 100 | 50 |
| 2 | Typing test on computer, English, 35 wpm | 10 minutes | 50 | 25 |
| 3 | Descriptive written test, English | 2 hours | 100 | 50 |
| 4 | Interview | – | 25 | 13 |

### Objective test: 100 questions

| Section | Questions |
|---|---|
| General English (including comprehension) | 20 |
| General Aptitude | 15 |
| General Knowledge | 15 |
| Constitution provisions on the Judiciary | 15 |
| Computer Knowledge | 15 |
| Supreme Court Rules, 2013 | 10 |
| Supreme Court Officers and Servants (Conditions of Service and Conduct) Rules, 1961 | 10 |

**0.25 marks are cut for each wrong answer.**

The 20 questions on the two sets of Supreme Court rules are where most candidates are weakest. The good news: the Supreme Court has put both rule books as PDFs on its recruitment page (notice dated 1 October 2026). Download them and read them properly. These 20 marks are the easiest to score if you prepare.

### Typing and descriptive tests

- **Typing:** 10 minutes, English, minimum 35 wpm. Mistakes up to 3% of the total words typed are allowed. A wrong word counts as a full mistake. A wrong capital or small letter, or a missing or wrong punctuation mark, counts as half a mistake.
- **Descriptive:** 2 hours in English, with a comprehension passage, précis writing and an essay.

Both of these are held **only in Delhi/NCR**. The interview is either online or in Delhi.

### How many get shortlisted?

If many candidates score 50+ in the objective test, the Registry will shortlist **10 candidates per vacancy** (about 2,500 for 250 posts) for the typing and descriptive tests. Your descriptive paper is checked **only if you pass the typing test**. Then **3 per vacancy** (about 750) go to the interview.

## Exam centres for the objective test

The objective test is held in **131 cities** across India. Some from the list:

- **Andhra Pradesh:** Guntur, Kakinada, Kurnool, Nellore, Rajahmundry, Tirupati, Vijayawada, Visakhapatnam, Vizianagaram
- **Telangana:** Hyderabad, Karimnagar, Khammam, Warangal
- **Karnataka:** Bengaluru, Mysuru, Hubballi, Mangaluru and more
- **Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Maharashtra, UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, the North-East** and others also have many centres.

You choose cities in order of preference while applying. The Registry can still move you to another city, and change requests aren't accepted.

| SC / ST / OBC (non-creamy layer) / Ex-servicemen / PwD | ₹750 + bank charges |

The fee is paid online through UCO Bank's payment gateway. It's **non-refundable**, even if you don't appear for the exam.

## How to apply for Supreme Court JCA 2026

1. Go to [sci.gov.in](https://www.sci.gov.in/recruitments/) → Recruitment. The application link goes live on **7 October 2026**.
2. Register with a mobile number and email you'll keep active till the end (all updates come by SMS and email).
3. Fill in your details. **Your name, father's name, mother's name and date of birth must match your 10th certificate exactly.**
4. Choose your category carefully. **You can't change it later.**
5. Pick your exam cities in order of preference.
6. Upload your photo, signature, and scans of your 10th, 12th and graduation certificates, plus caste and disability certificates if they apply.
7. Pay the fee online and submit.
8. Save the application number and take a printout. You don't need to post it anywhere.

Once submitted, the form **can't be edited or withdrawn**. If you submit more than one form, only the last one is considered.

If you already work in a central or state government office, a PSU or a nationalised bank, you'll need a **No Objection Certificate** from your employer at document verification.

## How to prepare in 6 weeks

- **Week 1 to 2:** Read the two Supreme Court rule books and the Judiciary part of the Constitution (Articles 124 to 147 for the Supreme Court, plus the High Court articles). That's 35 of the 100 questions.
- **Every day:** 20 to 30 minutes of typing practice in English. 35 wpm with less than 3% errors sounds easy, but exam keyboards and nerves slow you down. Aim for 40+ at home.
- **English and GK:** The same preparation as SSC CGL or CHSL works here. If you're also preparing for [SSC CHSL 2026](/ssc-chsl-2026-last-date), the overlap is big.
- **Computer knowledge:** Basics of MS Office, internet, shortcuts, hardware and software terms.
- **After the objective test:** Start précis and essay practice right away. The descriptive paper is in English and carries 100 marks.

## Should you apply?

Honestly, yes, if you're a graduate under 30 and can type. It's a respected Level 6 central job, the fee is the main cost, and the syllabus is mostly what you'd study for SSC anyway.

Two downsides to know before you pay ₹1,500. **The typing test, descriptive test and interview are in Delhi**, and no travel allowance is paid. If you're from the South or the North-East, plan for 2 trips. And the posting itself is in Delhi, so this isn't a job near home.

Skip it if your English writing is weak and you can't give it 6 weeks. The descriptive paper and the English-only typing test filter out a lot of people.

For any problem with the application, the Supreme Court Recruitment Cell's email is recruitment.cell@sci.nic.in. Bookmark the [official recruitment page](https://www.sci.gov.in/recruitments/) and check it every week for the admit card and exam updates.
